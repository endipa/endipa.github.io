19  How to contribute

Page team: Michele Dolce · Status: proposal, to be agreed with the editorial board · Text: demo text

Discuss on Zulip For project members: the Zulip and the wiki are by invitation. This page in the wiki (members) Propose a change TeX source PDF

This wiki is written from the bottom up, the way a journal works: members of the community propose changes on the talk page of the page they change, proposals are discussed and reviewed there in the open, an associate editor decides, and accepted changes are written into the wiki. The roles, and who holds them, are on Contributors. demo text

19.1 Roles

  • Editorial board: the scope and the structure of the wiki; appoints the associate editors; settles discussions that end without consensus.
  • Associate editors: one or more per part, following the talk pages of its pages and its Zulip channel. They run the discussion of each proposal, ask reviewers, and decide.
  • Reviewers: check a proposal at the request of an associate editor, in the open, on the talk page where it was made.
  • Community: propose and discuss. New members are endorsed by an editor or by a member at least two years past the PhD.

The first line of each page names its team. As the wiki grows, busy parts and pages get more associate editors and reviewers; the roles stay the same. On the wiki, everyone with a role is a member (reads every page and writes on talk pages); the editorial board and the associate editors are also editors (edit articles and templates, and write accepted proposals into the pages); administrators give these rights.

19.2 Proposing a change

Every change to a page, from a typo to a new proof, is a proposal: a topic on the talk page of the page (tab Discussion), which members of the wiki open and discuss. There are two ways to open one.

  • One paragraph (a typo, a sentence, a formula, a shorter argument): in the edition, the pencil button next to a paragraph shows its TeX. Change it, say why, and open the proposal: the wiki shows a new topic on the talk page, already filled in, to be saved.
  • Larger changes (a section, a statement, a proof, a new section): the link new proposal under the title of the page in the wiki (Propose a change in the edition) opens a new topic with a short form. Download the TeX source of the page: link TeX source under the title of every page of the edition (with the figures, in a zip, when the page has some). It is a standard LaTeX article that compiles as it is. In the Overleaf project of the whole wiki (Open the whole wiki in Overleaf, on the Overview) every page is a file of the folder pages, which can be proposed in the same way. Change what you want and check that it compiles; then paste in the form only the blocks you changed (a section, or a statement or a proof with its label), and say what they change, why, and which sources they use.

Those who are not yet members of the wiki write in the Zulip channel contributing, naming the page.

How a proposal is decided

  1. The community and the reviewers discuss the proposal in its topic, with replies that start with Support or Oppose, give a reason, and are signed. The author may revise the proposed text in place; the history of the talk page keeps every version.
  2. When the discussion has settled, an associate editor of the part decides: in the box at the top of the topic they change status=open to status=accepted, or to status=declined, and sign a reply that says why. There is no deadline. If the discussion ends without consensus, the editorial board decides.
  3. An editor writes the accepted proposal into the page with a script. The script converts the TeX to the wiki’s format, refuses a paragraph that has changed since the proposal was made, shows exactly what changes on the page, and flags new citations and links. The edit summary links the topic and credits its author, and the box of the topic becomes applied, with a link to the change.

Small corrections (a typo, a broken link, a missing reference) go the same way, and are usually accepted without discussion. Open and accepted proposals are listed in :Category:Open proposals and :Category:Accepted proposals. A proposal changes only the blocks it contains. A paper that is not yet on Literature is proposed on its own, on the talk page of the Literature page.

19.3 The TeX format

The TeX source of a page is a standard LaTeX article: one file that compiles with pdflatex and nothing else (on Overleaf, or with TeXShop, TeXstudio, …; typeset twice for the citations). Its preamble is the same for every page and is never changed; a contribution defines no macros, since the wiki has none. Sections and statements are numbered in the PDF only.

  • Statements: theorem, proposition, lemma, corollary, conjecture, definition, example, remark, claim. The optional argument is the name, the label is the anchor: \begin{theorem}[name]\label{thm-name}. Labels start with thm-, prp-, lem-, cor-, def- or clm-.
  • After a statement: \lean{} (the Lean banner), the environments idea and architecture, and proofs with an optional title and a label, as in \begin{proof}[Proof by hypocoercivity]\label{proof-name}. A claim and its proof sit inside the proof that uses them.
  • Formulas: $...$, \[ ... \] and align*; no numbered equations and no labels on equations; none of the commands that the wiki’s renderer rejects (the script lists them).
  • Citations: \citet{Key} gives Author (year) and \citep{Key} gives (Author, year), with the keys of Literature. The reference list at the end of the file is generated from the reference catalogue; a new citation is added to it when the proposal is accepted.
  • Links: on the same page, \hyperref[thm-name]{the theorem above} rather than \ref, since the wiki has no numbers; to another page, \wikilink{Page\#anchor}{text}.
  • Labels: \heuristic{}, \numerics{}, \published{}, \preprint{}, \raw{}.
  • A new block goes after an existing one: put the line %% after: Setting (a section title or a label) just before it.

19.4 Anatomy of a result

A result is a statement followed by its levels of detail, each folded until the reader opens it:

Statement in the simplest form, constants hidden

Lean a one-line banner: formalized or not yet

Idea the key mechanism in a few lines

Architecture the proof as a short chain of steps, later with a diagram

Proof the complete proof, in steps; a statement used only in this proof is a claim, with its own folded proof inside

A statement that several proofs use, or that holds in more generality than one result, goes to the Toolkit (Standard analysis or Enhanced-dissipation tools) and is linked from the proofs. A short proof is written in one piece. The specimen below shows the boxes at work; open the folds to see the nesting.

19.5 Labels

  • demo text: text drafted by Claude (AI), until a member of the page team replaces or approves it.
  • heuristic: a non-rigorous explanation, kept apart from what is proved.
  • numerics: a computation, with its record on Numerical methods.
  • published, preprint · to be verified, raw · unchecked: the status of an entry on Related results and Raw data: refereed; posted but not refereed; neither posted nor checked by a human (an AI-generated proof, a numerical observation, a sketch).
  • Grey quote boxes: AI-generated text, quoted verbatim with its source and date.

19.6 Pictures, videos and numerics

A picture, a video or a diagram is one line in the wiki, the template Media; in TeX, \media{video}{file}{caption}. The files are kept in the folder wiki/media of the project; the edition shows them, the wiki shows a box with the caption. Every computed picture or video has a record on Numerical methods, with the method, the parameters, the checks and the code.

19.7 Lean

Under each theorem a one-line banner says whether it is formalized: grey while it is not, green with a link to the certificate once it is. Certificates are listed on Lean formalization.

19.8 Discussion and conduct

Proposals are discussed on the talk page of the page they change, so that the discussion stays next to the text and in its history. Zulip is for everything else: questions, ideas for new results or new pages, announcements. It has one channel per part and one topic per page; the line under the title of each page links to its topic.

Associate editors moderate the channel and the talk pages of their part: they can hide messages and, after a warning, ask the editorial board to remove a member; their decisions can be appealed to the board. The Zulip is endipa.zulipchat.com, by invitation; its administrators invite new members and create channels.

19.9 Formulas in the wiki

For editors who write the wiki’s own format.

  • Inline formulas need display="inline", displayed ones display="block"; each formula is written on one line. The renderer rejects \newcommand, \label, \eqref and \substack. No formulas in headings.
  • Citations link to the Literature page by the key of the reference catalogue: [[Literature#BedrossianCotiZelati2017|Bedrossian and Coti Zelati (2017)]]. The section References of each page is generated from the catalogue: add the key to its comment line rather than editing the list.
  • To link to a result, use the id of its box as the anchor, as in Couette flow#thm-couette.

19.10 The edition

A static edition of this wiki, with the pictures, the videos and the TeX source of every page, is built from the wiki’s text by a script and never edited by hand: endipa.github.io.
