How to contribute

Contributors: Michele Dolce · Status: proposal, to be agreed with the editorial board · Text: demo text

For members: Discuss on Zulip This page in the wiki Propose a change TeX source PDF

This wiki is written from the bottom up, the way a journal works: members of the community propose changes on the talk page of the page they change, proposals are discussed and reviewed there in the open, an associate editor decides, and accepted changes are written into the wiki. The roles, and who holds them, are on Contributors. Short answers to practical questions are in the FAQ at the end of this page. demo text

Roles

  • Editorial board: the scope and the structure of the wiki; appoints the associate editors; settles discussions that end without consensus.
  • Associate editors: one or more per part, following the talk pages of its pages and its Zulip channel. They run the discussion of each proposal, ask members of the community for a review when it helps, and decide.
  • Community: propose, review and discuss. Every member can endorse a new member. Editors can remove a member whose participation disturbs the work rather than helping it.

The first line of each page names its contributors. As the wiki grows, busy parts and pages get more associate editors; the roles stay the same. On the wiki, everyone with a role is a member (reads every page and writes on talk pages); the editorial board and the associate editors are also editors (edit articles and templates, and write accepted proposals into the pages); administrators give these rights.

Proposing a change

Every change to a page, from a typo to a new proof, is a proposal: a topic on the talk page of the page (tab Discussion), which members of the wiki open and discuss. There are three ways to open one.

  • One paragraph, a figure, the central question, or a part still to be written (a typo, a sentence, a formula, a shorter argument, a caption, a shorter video, a missing proof): in the edition, the pencil button next to it shows its TeX or, for a part still to be written, an empty box (the link Write this part opens it too). Change the TeX (theorems, lemmas, remarks, proofs and formulas are written in TeX as usual, with \begin{lemma} ... \end{lemma}; the buttons of the box add a note, a folded box as on the Overview, or a claim, a dashed box), write the missing text, or describe the change you ask for (for a picture or a video, for instance), say why, and open the proposal: the wiki shows a new topic on the talk page, already filled in, to be saved. A text too long for a link is copied by the edition, to be pasted into the topic in the wiki.
  • Anything else on a page (a new box, another order, something to add, to move or to cut): the link Propose a change under the title of the page in the edition opens a box. Say in your own words what should change and, if you have it, give the new text in TeX; then open the proposal: the wiki shows the new topic, already filled in, to be saved. Once it is accepted, an editor writes the change into the page. For a whole new section or a long text, tick the last line of the box and attach the PDF to the topic in the wiki.
  • From the TeX source, for those who prefer to work on the file: the link new proposal under the title of the page in the wiki opens a new topic with a short form. Download the TeX source of the page: link TeX source under the title of every page of the edition (with the figures, in a zip, when the page has some). It is a standard LaTeX article that compiles as it is. The TeX of the whole wiki (TeX of the whole wiki, on the Overview) is one LaTeX project, for working offline, in which every page is a file of the folder pages; such a file can be proposed in the same way. Change what you want and check that it compiles; then paste in the form the blocks you changed (a section, or a statement or a proof with its label), or the whole file: only what changes is applied, and there is no limit of length. Say what the proposal changes, why, and which sources it uses; for a long proposal, attach its PDF (upload it and link it in the form).

Those who are not yet members of the wiki write in the Zulip channel contributing, naming the page.

How a proposal is decided

  1. The community discusses and reviews the proposal in its topic, with the buttons Support, Oppose and Comment of its box, which add a signed reply (the box counts the supports and the oppositions); replies can also be written by hand, signed with ~~~~. The author may revise the proposed text in place; the history of the talk page keeps every version.
  2. When the discussion has settled, an associate editor of the part decides, with the buttons Accept or Decline in the box at the top of the topic (shown to editors): the box changes status, and the reason they give is added as a signed reply. There is no deadline. If the discussion ends without consensus, the editorial board decides.
  3. An editor writes the accepted proposal into the page with a script. The script converts the TeX to the wiki’s format, refuses a paragraph that has changed since the proposal was made, shows exactly what changes on the page, and flags new citations and links. The edit summary links the topic and credits its author, and the box of the topic becomes applied, with a link to the change. Decided topics, applied or declined, are moved to the archive of the talk page (its subpage /Archive), so that the talk page shows only the proposals under discussion or waiting to be applied.

Small corrections (a typo, a broken link, a missing reference) go the same way, and are usually accepted without discussion. Open and accepted proposals are listed in :Category:Open proposals and :Category:Accepted proposals. A proposal changes only the blocks it contains. A paper that is not yet on Literature comes with the proposal that cites it (see Citations in The TeX format), and is added to the reference catalogue when the proposal is applied.

Credits

Under the title of a section, a line in small type names the people who contributed to it, the most recent first: {Contributed by|Name Surname, Name Surname} on the line after the heading (in TeX, \contributors{Name Surname, Name Surname} after the section title). A relevant contribution cannot be measured objectively: the editors decide, at their discretion, who is listed, and add a new name at the beginning of the list. A long list shows the three most recent names, and a link opens the others. The history of each page keeps the record of every change and of its author.

The TeX format

The TeX source of a page is a standard LaTeX article: one file that compiles with pdflatex and nothing else (on Overleaf, or with TeXShop, TeXstudio, …; typeset twice for the citations). Its preamble is the same for every page and is never changed; a contribution defines no macros, since the wiki has none. Sections are numbered in the PDF only; the website numbers the results within each page (Theorem 1, Lemma 2, …).

  • Statements: theorem, proposition, lemma, corollary, conjecture, definition, example, remark, claim. The optional argument is the name, the label is the anchor: \begin{theorem}[name]\label{thm-name}. Labels start with thm-, prp-, lem-, cor-, def-, clm-, rem- (remarks), cnj- or exm-; a statement sent without a label gets one made from its name or its first words.
  • After a statement: the environments idea and architecture, and proofs with an optional title and a label, as in \begin{proof}[Proof by hypocoercivity]\label{proof-name}. A claim and its proof sit inside the proof that uses them.
  • Formulas: $...$, \[ ... \] and align*; no numbered equations and no labels on equations; none of the commands that the wiki’s renderer rejects (the script lists them).
  • Citations: \citet{Key} gives Author (year) and \citep{Key} gives (Author, year), with the keys of Literature; several papers at once: \citep{Key1,Key2,Key3}. The reference list at the end of each page is generated from the reference catalogue and contains only the papers that the page cites: a new citation is added to it when the proposal is applied. A paper that is not yet on Literature: cite it with a key of your choice, such as AuthorYear, and give its reference in the field New references of the proposal, one line per paper, \bibitem{AuthorYear} Authors, title, journal volume (year), pages, with its DOI or arXiv number; it is added to the catalogue and then to the page.
  • Links: on the same page, \hyperref[thm-name]{the theorem above} rather than \ref, since the wiki has no numbers; to another page, \wikilink{Page\#anchor}{text}.
  • Labels: \heuristic{}, \numerics{}, \published{}, \preprint{}, \raw{}.
  • A new block goes after an existing one: put the line %% after: Setting (a section title or a label) just before it.

Anatomy of a result

A result is a statement followed by its levels of detail, each folded until the reader opens it:

Statement in the simplest form, constants hidden

Idea the key mechanism in a few lines

Architecture the proof as a short chain of steps, later with a diagram

Proof the complete proof: first its sources (the papers it follows, cited by their key), then the steps; a statement used only in this proof is a claim, with its own folded proof inside

A statement that several proofs use, or that holds in more generality than one result, goes to the Toolkit (Standard analysis or Enhanced-dissipation tools) and is linked from the proofs. A short proof is written in one piece. The specimen below shows the boxes at work; open the folds to see the nesting.

Labels

  • demo text: text drafted by Claude (AI), until a contributor of the page replaces or approves it.
  • heuristic: a non-rigorous explanation, kept apart from what is proved.
  • numerics: a computation, with its record on Numerical methods.
  • published, preprint · to be verified, raw · unchecked: the status of an entry on Related results and Raw data: refereed; posted but not refereed; neither posted nor checked by a human (an AI-generated proof, a numerical observation, a sketch).
  • Grey quote boxes: AI-generated text, quoted verbatim with its source and date.

Provenance and AI use

Where each piece of the wiki comes from, and who has checked what; the labels are those of the section above. demo text

  • Text drafted by Claude (AI) carries demo text until a contributor of the page replaces or approves it.
  • Arguments produced by AI systems appear only as verbatim quotations, with source and date, or as entries labelled raw · unchecked on Raw data.
  • The first line of each page records its contributors, its status and the checks made. A machine check is recorded as such and never counts as a human check.
  • Statements attributed to a paper give its theorem number; synthesis proofs say which papers they follow.
  • Every accepted proposal is linked, in the summary of the edit that applies it, to the topic of the talk page where it was discussed.

Pictures, videos and numerics

A picture, a video or a diagram is one line in the wiki, the template Media; in TeX, \media{video}{file}{caption}. The files are kept in the folder wiki/media of the project; the edition shows them, the wiki shows a box with the caption. Every computed picture or video has a record on Numerical methods, with the method, the parameters, the checks and the code.

A proposal that adds or replaces a picture or a video names the file in its \media line; the file itself is sent separately, since the text of a proposal cannot carry it. A picture (png, svg, jpg or pdf) is uploaded to the wiki and linked in the topic of the proposal. A video (mp4), which the wiki does not accept, is attached to a message in the Zulip topic of the page, where the other members can watch it before the proposal is decided; the topic of the proposal gives the link to that message. For a computed picture or video, attach to the same message the code that produced it, with its parameters and its checks: they make its record on Numerical methods. When the proposal is accepted, an editor adds the files to the edition together with the text. demo text

Discussion and conduct

Proposals are discussed on the talk page of the page they change, so that the discussion stays next to the text and in its history. Zulip is the forum for the mathematics: questions and clarifications, ideas for new results and how they fit the established theory, and larger changes to the structure of the wiki, discussed there before they become proposals. It has one channel per part and one topic per question or idea; the link under the title of each page opens the topic named after the page, for questions about it.

About this wiki contributing

Discuss the mathematics, not the people. Associate editors moderate the channel and the talk pages of their part, and can hide messages; editors can remove a member whose participation disturbs the work rather than helping it. The Zulip is endipa.zulipchat.com, by invitation; its administrators invite new members and create channels.

Formulas in the wiki

For editors who write the wiki’s own format.

  • Inline formulas need display="inline", displayed ones display="block"; each formula is written on one line. The renderer rejects \newcommand, \label, \eqref and \substack. No formulas in headings.
  • Citations link to the Literature page by the key of the reference catalogue: [[Literature#BedrossianCotiZelati2017|Bedrossian and Coti Zelati (2017)]]. The section References of each page is generated from the catalogue: add the key to its comment line rather than editing the list.
  • To link to a result, use the id of its box as the anchor, as in Couette flow#thm-couette.

The edition

A static edition of this wiki, with the pictures, the videos and the TeX source of every page, is built from the wiki’s text by a script and never edited by hand: endipa.github.io.

FAQ

Practical questions, answered briefly; the list grows with the wiki. demo text

NoteHow do I propose a change to one paragraph?
  1. Open the page in the edition and move the pointer over the paragraph, or the figure: a pencil button appears on its right. On a phone or a tablet the button is always shown.
  2. Click it: a box opens with the TeX of the paragraph. Change the TeX, and check the result in the preview below it.
  3. In the field below the preview, say in one line why. To ask for a change without writing it (a clearer picture, a shorter video), leave the TeX as it is and describe the change in that field. If the text cites a paper that is not on the Literature page, the box names it: give its reference in the field New references.
  4. Click Open the proposal on the talk page. The wiki opens in a new tab (log in if it asks), with a new topic on the talk page of the page, already filled in: check it and click Save changes.
  5. The other members reply in the topic with Support, Oppose or Comment, and an associate editor accepts or declines. An editor then writes the accepted change into the page, and it appears in the edition when this is next rebuilt.

On a part still to be written, the same button opens an empty box for the missing text. For a whole section, a statement or a proof, see Proposing a change.
